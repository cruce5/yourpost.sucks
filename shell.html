What decides the score

A fixed checklist, in code, the same every time. It never asks AI for anything, so the score is identical whether AI is on, off or broken. Five categories. Open one to see its checks.

What AI adds

When available, AI writes the commentary, the one-line verdict and the rewrite. It gets the finished score and cannot change it. Everything it writes is checked first. It can't:

  • state or imply a score
  • predict reach
  • put a number in a rewrite that your post didn't contain
  • say "as an AI"
  • use an em dash

It can still get a name or a detail wrong, so read a rewrite before you post it. Anything that fails is swapped for the checklist's version. The badge by the score says who wrote the commentary. It is not a verdict on whether your post is AI-written.

When it says no

A post about a death, serious illness, self-harm, abuse, violence or pregnancy loss is declined before anything else runs. No AI, no score, no cache. It errs toward declining on purpose.

What happens to what you paste

The post and any image go to Anthropic's API for the commentary. The AI's notes are cached for 30 days. Your post is not. An hourly per-IP counter limits AI reports and expires on its own. No accounts, no analytics on your text.

What's new

All of it came from things people said in the comments.

  • You can ask for a meaner write-up. Tick Be meaner about it under the box. The score does not change and the checks do not change, only how bluntly the report talks about the writing. A red bar across the top tells you it is on.
  • Pasting a link no longer gets you a nonsense score. Paste the words of the post, not its web address. If you paste a link, the page now says so instead of scoring the link.
  • The rewrite keeps your voice. It used to re-say your whole post in smoother language. Now it changes only the parts a check flagged and leaves your sentences alone.
  • An acronym in a citation is not shouting. Something like SANER 2025 was being scored as if you were yelling. It is not any more.
  • Small print. The report explains why a clean post scores 0.2 and not 0.0, and the site finally has its own icon.
