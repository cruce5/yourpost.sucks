# Post Craft Rules

Structural rules for editing any LinkedIn post, drawn from one creator's corpus. **Whatever register the writer is in (earnest, technical, wry, blunt) make it land harder. Never swap it.**

---

## 1. CRAFT PRINCIPLES

**1. Enter flat and cold.** Open on a statement, quote, or concrete detail. Never a question.
*Evidence:* Flat declaratives dominate the top tier (4,967; 2,580; 1,853); a rhetorical setup question flopped at 46. Named dead openers: "I've been thinking about…"
*Before:* "Ever wonder why discharge takes so long?" → *After:* "The patient is ready in twenty minutes. The paperwork takes four hours."

**2. Lead with the human, not the label.** Bury the org or event name; open on the person or claim.
*Evidence:* A program's name as the opener: 51 and 61. The same program, led by one person's decision to quit her job: 238, roughly 4x, same promotional intent.
*Before:* "Our Hospitality Summit is Tuesday. Register now." → *After:* "A front desk agent talked a guest out of a one-star review. She speaks Tuesday."

**3. Name the specific.** Replace abstractions with an exact number, time, or object.
*Evidence:* "Tuesday at 4:58 pm" (1,427), "checked it 43 times" (2,580). A post whose payoff was the word "disappointing" scored 46, too vague.
*Before:* "A complicated shift change caused problems." → *After:* "Three nurses handed off eleven patients using one dying whiteboard marker."

**4. Complete the thought.** Short is fine; fragmentary is not. Every post needs its second half.
*Evidence:* A two-line setup/payoff hit 1,515; same-length fragments died (48, 42). Length follows structure: escalations need 500–700 characters (2,837), reversals under 150 words (2,580).
*Before:* "Contractors, you know what I mean." → *After:* "Contractors: the client picks the tile after the tile is installed."

**5. Stand alone from the attachment.** The text must hold up with the image or link removed.
*Evidence:* Two words over a surprising image: 898 eng / 147K impressions. Visual-dependent captions collapsed (50, 41, 40).
*Before:* "😂 so true" [meme] → *After:* "Someone laminated the break room microwave rules. All nine of them." [photo]

**6. Escalate in plausible steps.** Each beat outdoes the last while staying believable.
*Evidence:* A recursive escalation post hit 2,837: every turn worse, all of it realistic, and readers extended the chain in comments.
*Before:* "Retail in December is chaos." → *After:* "Dec 1: we build the display. Dec 8: a customer rebuilds it. Dec 15: he's training other customers."

**7. Land smaller than the setup.** End on understatement, not a summary or moral.
*Evidence:* The top post (4,967) ends a career-change narrative with "Seems to be working out on both fronts." Another closes on "Probably user error." (865). Over-explaining is a named failure mode.
*Before:* "…and that's why leadership is about listening. Thoughts?" → *After:* "She'd said it in every meeting for a year. We just wrote it down this time."

**8. Make the last line add something.** A closer must reframe or extend; if it restates, cut it.
*Evidence:* Reframing closers hit 1,746 and 1,479. Where the body already landed, the two that close without one (4,967; 1,445) did fine. A closer that only plugs something: 98.
*Before:* "…and that's how we cut wait times. Efficiency matters!" → *After:* "…and that's how we cut wait times. We also lost the fish tank."

**9. Hold one register.** Don't graft a punchline onto a sincere post, or sincerity onto a light one.
*Evidence:* The two straight posts (4,967; 1,445) end without a punchline; forcing one into an earnest post is a named failure. Mixing breeds hedging: "I think maybe," "sort of." Cut those.
*Before:* "My grandmother passed last month. Anyway, here's what that taught me about procurement." → *After:* "My grandmother passed last month. She taught me to weigh flour, not scoop it. I still do."

**10. Build participation in, don't bolt it on.** Engagement comes from structure readers want to extend or argue with.
*Evidence:* The escalation post drew comments extending it (2,837); one line over a divisive image drew 235. The bolted-on ask scored 51.
*Before:* "Best budgeting tip for small restaurants? Comment below!" → *After:* "Every restaurant has one dish quietly subsidizing the menu. Ours is the side salad."

**11. Assume zero prior context.** Any in-group reference needs an on-ramp for a stranger.
*Evidence:* Context-dependent posts died: a second-language post (42), a video-dependent post (41), a recap assuming familiarity (51).
*Before:* "Another SOAP note battle with the EHR today." → *After:* "Charting a fifteen-minute visit takes twenty-two minutes. The software was sold as a time-saver."

**12. Delete the institutional voice.** Cut anything that sounds drafted by a comms team.
*Evidence:* The avoid-list ends on posts that sound "written by a marketing team." Logistics-only posts are the worst category (51, 61).
*Before:* "Thrilled to announce a strategic initiative to elevate the customer journey." → *After:* "We stopped making people call to cancel. It took two years to approve."

## 2. DIAGNOSTIC QUESTIONS

Run in order on the post text alone; each "no" flags a principle.

1. Is the first line a statement, quote, or concrete detail, not a question or announcement? → P1
2. Does the first sentence offer a stranger something interesting, not an org or event name? → P2
3. Does the post contain at least one exact number, time, name, or object? → P3
4. Does it have both a setup and a payoff? Would it work alone on screen? → P4
5. If an image, video, or link is referenced, is the text still interesting without it? → P5
6. With three or more beats, does each raise stakes while staying believable? → P6
7. Is the final line smaller and more concrete than the build-up, not a summary? → P7
8. Does the final line say something the body didn't? → P8
9. Does the post hold one register, with no hedges ("I think maybe," "sort of")? → P9
10. Is engagement invited by structure rather than an appended question? → P10
11. Could a first-time reader follow every reference? → P11
12. Is it free of announcement-speak and logistics with no reason to care? → P12

---

## 3. EDIT TYPES

- **Rewrite the opening line:** *Q1 fails.* Pull the most concrete sentence up front; make questions declarative. Give the line verbatim.
- **Bury the lede:** *Q2 fails.* Move the org or event name later in the post.
- **Replace an abstraction with a specific:** *Q3 fails.* Name the vague word, ask for the fact ("'a long wait': how long?"). Supply a slot, never a number.
- **Add the missing half:** *Q4 fails.* Say whether setup or payoff is missing; supply its shape.
- **Make the text attachment-independent:** *Q5 fails.* Give the caption its own claim; keep it short.
- **Reorder for escalation:** *Q6 fails.* Sequence beats weakest to strongest; dial back any that breaks plausibility.
- **Cut the final line or paragraph:** *Q7/Q8 fail.* End it a line or two earlier; say what the cut was doing.
- **Rewrite the closer to add a beat:** *Q8 fails, closer wanted.* Propose a line that reframes, deflates, or extends.
- **Cut the hedge / restore the register:** *Q9 fails.* Delete qualifiers; remove the graft, don't rewrite the passage.
- **Remove the engagement ask:** *Q10 fails.* Replace with a contestable claim or open pattern.
- **Add the on-ramp:** *Q11 fails.* One clause of context for the jargon; never delete the reference.
- **Strip institutional voice:** *Q12 fails.* Rewrite plainly, no longer; put a reason-to-care sentence ahead of logistics.
- **Cut repetition:** *bloat.* Remove sentences restating a neighbor; never cut toward a word target.

---

## 4. ANTI-GUIDANCE

Never suggest:

- **A tone the writer isn't already using.** The top two posts (4,967; 4,627) and the layoff post (1,445) are played entirely straight. Register is the writer's; craft is ours.
- **Hashtags, decorative emoji, "thoughts?", "comment below."** Named failure devices; the appended-ask post scored 51.
- **An inspirational or lesson-shaped ending.** The best endings here are deliberately anticlimactic.
- **A rhetorical question opener.** The most consistently documented underperformer.
- **Hype vocabulary:** "thrilled," "game-changing," "unlock," "leverage," "journey."
- **Invented specifics.** Ask for the real number; these details land because they're true.
- **Polishing away imperfect phrasing.** "Authentic over polished" is explicit; the top post uses casual bullets and a mid-sentence ellipsis.
- **Removing a distinctive recurring element** (a signature closer or running theme) as "unprofessional." A callback post hit 1,479. Flag it only if it restates the body or only plugs something (98).
- **Lengthening a short post that's already complete.** 1,515 engagements on two lines.
- **Credential lists, frameworks, or numbered "key takeaways."** Lists need a narrative engine.
- **Optimizing for impressions.** One post drew 98K impressions on 232 engagements; reach is not resonance.
- **Softening a committed claim.** Full commitment landed at 1,479; the post that hedged its premise scored 46.
